# 2026-10-27: calibration as the score that no insider gets to judge

## The question (Will)
"How do we get a good scoring rule if we'll be the ones judging it? What's the unbiased score for a successful Noesis? To a planet it's homeostasis."

## The reasoning
- **Every inside judge is biased toward what it selected.** Oxygen is the example: it devastated the anaerobic life of its time, and we call it good because we are among the organisms it selected (Will's correction to the paper's use of the example). A scoring rule will tend to look fair to the contributors it rewards.
- **A planet escapes this because homeostasis is not a judgment.** It is a physical variable held in bounds against conditions the planet does not control, and the verdict is persistence.
- **So the score must be future, external, and checkable.** Candidate adopted: calibration, how well valuations today predict independent use later. The regulated variable is the gap between what the system values and what outsiders later do.

## The fractal form (candidate claim T5)
The same regulated variable appears to recur at every scale: an organism's internal state against its environment; a brain's prediction error (predictive processing, a standard account not yet checked here); a market's prices against later scarcity; a contribution economy's prices against later use; a planet's surface conditions against what life tolerates. If T5 holds, health at every scale is calibration, and T4 (worth is relational) becomes measurable: relational worth is what the outside world later does with an item.

## Where independence draws its line (Will: "true independence doesn't exist, so there's a line somewhere")
Independence is a price, not a fact. An outcome counts to the degree that faking it would cost more than the reward it unlocks. Will confirmed this line and noted it is the same one blockchain game theory draws: a system is secure when an attack costs more than it earns. Nature's version: a legume responds to nitrogen actually delivered, which is physically costly to fake (Kiers et al. 2003).

## Known failure modes
Fake outsiders (the grounding problem); self-fulfilling scores (high scores bring exposure, so randomize exposure for a slice); lag (fast proxies stay off the money path).
