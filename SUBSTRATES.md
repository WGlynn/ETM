# Substrates: the same economy at other scales

ETM's thesis T3 says the structure shared by minds and economies repeats at every scale ([PROGRAM.md](PROGRAM.md)). This file extends the comparison beyond minds and markets, to organisms, colonies, cities, and the planet, where biology and physics have already done careful work. Every comparison here is a candidate for the transfer ledger, not a conclusion.

Citation status: **checked** means the citation and the quoted claim were checked against a publisher, journal, or index listing on 2026-10-27. **To check** means a standard result, cited from general knowledge, not yet checked here. ## Checked anchors

### Cancer as cheating (organism scale)
Aktipis CA, Boddy AM, Jansen G, Hibner U, Hochberg ME, Maley CC, Wilkinson GS (2015). Cancer across the tree of life: cooperation and cheating in multicellularity. *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B* 370: 20140219. doi:10.1098/rstb.2014.0219. Book-length treatment: Aktipis (2020), *The Cheating Cell*, Princeton University Press.

The paper defines cancer as a breakdown of five "foundations of multicellularity": proliferation inhibition, cell death, division of labour, resource allocation, and extracellular environment maintenance. It defines cheating as "the breakdown of shared rules ... that leads to a fitness advantage on the cellular level for the cheater."

**Transfer candidate (X5):** the five foundations as an audit checklist for any economy of contributors, mind-like or protocol. Proliferation inhibition maps to Sybil resistance (uncontrolled copies of an identity). Cell death maps to enforced exit (rent). Division of labour maps to role separation. Resource allocation maps to the pricing rule. Environment maintenance maps to upkeep of shared state. Prediction: in a contribution economy, each known exploit breaks at least one foundation, and a foundation with no enforcement is where the next exploit will appear.

### Major transitions: a level exists when its parts cannot cheat it
Maynard Smith J, Szathmary E (1995). *The Major Transitions in Evolution*. Freeman / Oxford. Companion paper: Szathmary E, Maynard Smith J (1995). The major evolutionary transitions. *Nature* 374: 227-232. Maynard Smith's summary: "In each of these revolutions, there has been a conflict between selection at several levels. The achievement of individuality at the higher level has required that the disruptive effects of selection at the lower level be suppressed." Later work names complete repression of competition within groups as one route, with worker policing of eggs in some social insects as an example (West et al., *PNAS* 2015).

**Transfer candidate (X6):** this gives T3 its precise condition. Genes to cells to organisms to colonies: each new "mind of minds" exists only to the degree its parts cannot profit at its expense. Translated to ETM, T2 becomes a matter of degree: **an economy is a mind to the degree it suppresses cheating by its parts.** That is measurable in principle (how much of the system's reward a cheater can capture), and it unifies the wash (section 6 of PROGRAM), Hayek's property rights (X1), and cancer (X5) as one phenomenon at different levels.

### Scaling laws: the structure transfers, the dynamics can flip
West GB, Brown JH, Enquist BJ (1997), *Science*: Kleiber's law (metabolic rate scales with mass to the 3/4 power) derived from fractal branching distribution networks. Bettencourt LMA, Lobo J, Helbing D, Kuhnert C, West GB (2007). Growth, innovation, scaling, and the pace of life in cities. *PNAS* 104(17): 7301-7306. doi:10.1073/pnas.0610172104. Abstract: quantities reflecting wealth creation and innovation scale with exponent about 1.2 (increasing returns); infrastructure about 0.8 (economies of scale). The paper itself discusses how cities resemble and differ from organisms, for which the exponent is below 1.

**Limit on T3 (X7):** this is the strongest measured "as above, so below" in the literature, and it cuts both ways. The *form* repeats: power laws arising from network structure, in organisms and in cities. The *dynamics* flip: organisms grow to a bound and slow down; cities' economic output grows without bound and speeds up, which the authors argue requires ever-faster cycles of innovation to avoid collapse. ETM must state which of its claims are about structure and which about dynamics, and must not assume a dynamic transfers just because a structure does. Note: the often-quoted 1.15 and 0.85 are later rounded values; the 2007 abstract says about 1.2 and 0.8.

### Gaia: a planet as a homeostatic controller, and its cheater problem
Lovelock JE, Margulis L (1974). Atmospheric homeostasis by and for the biosphere: the Gaia hypothesis. *Tellus* 26: 2-10. doi:10.1111/j.2153-3490.1974.tb01946.x. They propose "the biosphere as an active adaptive control system able to maintain the earth in homeostasis." The standing objection (Dawkins; Doolittle 1981) is the cheater problem: an organism that takes the benefit of planetary regulation without paying its cost should spread, and a planet is "a population of one" that cannot be selected among. Doolittle later revised his view (*Darwinizing Gaia*, MIT Press, 2024).

**Transfer candidate (X8):** ETM inherits Gaia's objection exactly. A single economy, or a single chain, is also a population of one. Whatever answer biology accepts for how planetary regulation resists cheaters without selection among planets is a candidate answer for how one global economy, or one protocol, could. Status: open on both sides.

## Nature's solution library (checked)

The working hypothesis behind this section: nature has been running contribution economies under cheating pressure for a very long time, and its solutions are the first place to look before designing new ones. Caution, stated once and applied throughout: nature's solutions are not free or perfect (cancer still happens; an immune system that overreacts attacks its own body), and evolution tuned them by selecting among many populations, which a single economy does not have (X8). A solution found in nature is a candidate to test, not a proof.

### Host sanctions: punish per unit of output, not per individual
Kiers ET, Rousseau RA, West SA, Denison RF (2003). Host sanctions and the legume-rhizobium mutualism. *Nature* 425: 78-81. doi:10.1038/nature01931. When rhizobia were prevented from fixing nitrogen, their reproductive success fell by about 50%, at three spatial scales: whole plants, half root systems, and individual nodules. Reduced oxygen supply to non-fixing nodules was the likely mechanism.

**Transfer candidate (X9):** the host monitors and sanctions at the finest unit of output (the nodule), not at the level of the partner as a whole. Translated: a contribution economy should price per unit of realized output, never per identity. Prediction: any rule that pays per account or per identity (a flat floor, a per-member share) will be exploitable by splitting, and the fix is to move the price down to the unit of output. Observed on the economy side: a VibeSwap reward floor paid per account was found profitable to split in 200 of 200 adversarial search rounds (verified in a code comment; see the notebook).

### Reciprocal rewards: control in both directions keeps a market honest
Kiers ET et al. (2011). Reciprocal rewards stabilize cooperation in the mycorrhizal symbiosis. *Science* 333: 880-882. doi:10.1126/science.1208473. Plants detect and reward the fungal partners that supply more nutrients with more carbohydrates; the fungi in turn send more nutrients to the roots that supply more carbohydrates. Neither side can be "enslaved"; the authors attribute stability to control running both ways.

**Consistency (X10):** Noesis finality requires both its capital side and its contribution side to supply at least half of their own totals, so neither can finalize without the other (Noesis `node/src/runtime.rs:1664,1676` at commit `be219951`). That is the same structure: two-way control. Status: consistency only; both sides were known before this entry.

### Biological markets: when choosiness works
Noe R, Hammerstein P (1994). Biological markets: supply and demand determine the effect of partner choice in cooperation, mutualism and mating. *Behavioral Ecology and Sociobiology* 35: 1-11. doi:10.1007/bf00167053. Cooperation is decided by comparing offers from several potential partners. When one class sufficiently outnumbers the other and sampling offers is cheap for the choosing class, choosiness selects for partners that supply high-value commodities.

**Transfer candidate (X11):** this states the conditions under which "let users choose" defeats the wash. Prediction for any contribution economy, Noesis's designed periphery included: choice by independent users prices out worthless contributions only when (a) users outnumber contributors enough and (b) judging a contribution is cheap for a user. Where judging is expensive, choosiness fails and the wash survives. This turns a design hope into two measurable conditions.

## To check next (standard results, not yet checked here)

- **Clonal selection** (Burnet). Immune cells whose receptors bind a target multiply; credit by use. Tolerance, the removal of self-reactive cells, is cheater suppression.
- **Synaptic pruning** ("use it or lose it").
- **Hayek, *The Sensory Order* (1952).** The same author who described prices as a distributed mind also wrote a theory of the mind as a classifying network. Both sides of ETM's thesis, one author.
